Method

The pipeline

Every stage was chosen from data-quality evidence: digit-level tokenization, exact-division-only generation, backward-constructed multi-op lines, and a 20k-line automated audit (0 errors).

01 · DATA

Synthetic corpus

3M lines of compact add/subtract, 2–4 digit operands, uniform sampling, deduped. Reversed-answer variant at 50% for run 3.

math_corpus_generator.py · audited
02 · TOKENIZER

14-token digit vocab

0–9 + − = <eos> — every digit is one token so carry/borrow propagation is one decision per step. <eos> is a single terminal token.

no BPE — no subword merge
03 · MODEL

Modern stack, 5.12M

6 layers · d=256 · 8 heads · RMSNorm · RoPE · SwiGLU · QK-norm · tied embeddings. Reference: llm-modern-arch-experiment.

ModernGPT
04 · TRAIN

Muon + AdamW, bf16

32,768 tokens/update · 2000 steps · warmup 50 · cosine→0.1 · wd 0.1. ~700s per 66M-token run on one GPU at 96% util.

What changed

Six more findings

07
🧱

The wall moves, it never lifts

Training on 2–9 digits took 5–6 digit accuracy from 0% to 81.85% — the run-3 wall was a data gap, not a capability limit. But 10–12 digit stayed at exactly 0.00% across all four runs and a 5× token sweep. Extend the range and the wall follows it.

08
🔚

A second wall at the short end

Single-digit problems were never generated (--min-digits 2), and every model scored 0%. The cause is not arithmetic: on 1-digit answers the first emitted digit is correct 91/91 = 100%, but <eos> ranks ~10th, so it cannot stop — 1+2= → 3633. It computes perfectly and runs on.

09
📐

Filling the short end lifts everything

Adding 1-digit operands gained +14 to +22 points at every answer length, not just short ones: 2–9d 70.80→86.25%, 5–6d 59.25→81.85%, 2–4d 47.50→61.90%. The cheapest single change in the whole study.

10
⚠️

Never select checkpoints by loss

In run 6, ckpt_final beat the loss-selected ckpt_best by 8–10 points on every split while being 0.0005 worse on eval loss — noise, in a curve flat to ±0.005 for its last 2000 steps. Loss and accuracy diverge here; loss-based selection picks at random.

11
⚖️

More tokens are not uniformly better

100 tpp beat 20 tpp on 5–6 digit (80.85 vs 59.25%) but lost ground on 2–4 digit (34.85 vs 47.50%). At 5.1M params the model reallocates capacity toward wherever the data mass sits. This is a capacity ceiling, not a data problem.

12
🚀

The trainer was 7× too slow

torch.compile was disabled on a wrong assumption, and every step shuffled a 258k-element list to pick 128. Fixed: 53.7k → 372k tok/s, a 20-tpp run in 276s. Caveat: the 14-token vocab is itself worth 1.53× versus a 16k-vocab reference — at dim 256 the lm_head is 45% of forward FLOPs at V=16,384 but 0.07% at V=14.

Run 8

Adding multiplication and division

Same recipe as run 7 (1–9 digit, 20 tpp, 287s), with mul and div in the corpus. This required a vocab change, 14 → 16 tokens (x and /), which makes every earlier checkpoint unloadable. Addition, subtraction and division survive the move. Multiplication does not.

What run 8 showed

Three more findings

13
✖️

Multiplication is a different problem

5.20% versus 60–83% for the other three ops. And it is not the length wall: mul gets the length right 54% of the time but the first digit right only 12% — it knows how big the answer is and cannot compute it. 9x9 → 1011. Addition is digit-local with a carry; multiplication needs every partial product at once. A 5M digit-level decoder does not do that.

14
➗

Exact division is easier than multiplication

60.53% — close to addition, nowhere near mul. The generator builds division backward (a = q·d, d in 2–9), so recovering q is closer to a digit-local scan than to a full product. The corpus construction, not the operation, sets the difficulty.

15
🕳️

A held-out set can be contaminated by construction

The 10–12 digit test scored 9.67% — apparently the first crack in the wall. It was an artifact: --min-digits 10 bounds the dividend, but division is built as a = q·d with small d, so quotients land back inside the trained range. Restricted to genuine 10–12 digit add/sub, the score is 0.00%. The wall stands across five runs.

Runs 9–10

The short-digit wall was a formatting bug

Run 9 narrowed the corpus to 1–4 digits at 100 tpp. It scored 60.15% on the original 2–4 digit eval — still below run 3's 78.70%, with 2-digit answers at 1%. The digits were right; the model would not stop. 40 seconds of SFT — 0.114% of the pretraining budget — fixed it.

What SFT showed

Three final findings

16
🛑

Packed pretraining never teaches stopping

Loss over a packed stream makes <eos> one token among millions, never the target of a distinct example. Masking the prompt to -100 and supervising only the completion — the recipe from llm-modern-arch-experiment, whose SFT exists for this exact reason — took the 2–4 digit eval from 60.15% to 99.05% in 1000 updates. That finally beats run 3's 78.70%, by 20 points.

17
🔀

SFT also collapsed the reversed/forward ambiguity

Strict (forward-order-only) accuracy went 21.55% → 99.05%, exactly matching lenient. Every earlier run leaned on a scorer that accepted either order, because 50/50 reversed training left the model emitting a mix. After SFT the two metrics are identical: it emits forward order every time. 7+5= → 21 became 12.

18
🧱

SFT fixes format, not capability

5–6 digit stayed at exactly 0.00% before and after. Supervising the completion teaches when to stop and which order to emit; it cannot teach arithmetic the model never learned. That cleanly separates the two walls: the short-side one was a formatting artifact and is gone, the long-side one is a real capacity limit and is untouched.

What we learned

Four findings

Loss decomposition: first answer digit costs 2.44 nats (near-uniform over 14), the rest 1.23 (ln 14 = 2.64). That single number explains everything below.

01
⏹

<eos> stopping is free & robust

100% stop rate in all three runs, all lengths. The terminal-token objective is the cheapest capability in the whole corpus — it generalizes perfectly.

02
🔁

Reversed answers: +12.5 pts, zero cost

Training 50% of lines with reversed answer digits aligns left-to-right decoding with right-to-left carry propagation. 66.15% → 78.70% exact-match. Keep it.

03
🧱

Length generalization is the wall

0% on 5–6 digit inputs in every run, at 10× params of tokens. A 5M causal decoder learned digit-level statistics, not column-wise arithmetic. The attention pattern carry propagation needs (digit attending to its right-hand neighbor) fights the decoding direction.

04
⚡

Modern stack + Muon is the right tool

704s vs 958s at 2× data, flat loss curve to step 2000 (no overfit) vs run 1's overfit after ~epoch 18. bf16 + 32K tokens/update saturated the GPU at 96%.

05
📏

Failures are boundary errors

Run 3's mistakes are almost all length/termination errors — right digits, wrong count. The model knows the arithmetic; it doesn't know when the number ends.

06
🎯

Good data ≠ more data

Run 1 proved the budget principle: 33.5M tokens of an overfit fp32 run ≈ 66M tokens of a well-optimized one. Curation and recipe beat raw volume at this scale.
